Himalayan Expedition Underwriting Platform

Domain: Underwriting Risk Tech Stack: Power BI, SQL, Python Project Scale: 11,425 Expeditions

1. Business Challenge

Underwriting high-altitude adventure insurance represents extreme pricing hazards for insurance corporations. Due to irregular historical recording systems and unpredictably fluctuating casualty variables (weather triggers, crew sizes, oxygen availability), underwriting premiums were priced on basic intuitive estimates rather than actuary calculations. This led to severe underwriting losses on dangerous peaks like Annapurna, while pricing out potential climbers on safer routes.

2. Data Sources & Integration

Aggregated and normalized 119 years of mountaineering histories from the Himalayan Database (1890-2026). Normalized irregular entries and constructed a schema storing:

Expeditions Master ID, Peak, Year, Season, Success Rate, Oxygen used, Support count
Climbers Registry ID, Age, Gender, Role, Nationality, Death status, Injury details

3. Analytical Approach

I engineered a relational SQL schema to link climbers to their parent expedition entities. A data cleaning pipeline in Python (Pandas) handled null values across missing decades, and isolated age groups. Advanced actuary calculations were modeled in Power BI using custom DAX parameters to measure the rolling mortality index.

An example of the DAX formula designed to establish the High Altitude Fatality Index (HAFI) across Peak Groups:

HAFI_Index =
VAR TotalClimbers = CALCULATE(COUNT(Climbers[ID]))
VAR Fatalities = CALCULATE(COUNT(Climbers[ID]), Climbers[Status] = "Deceased")
VAR AltitudeWeight = IF(SELECTEDVALUE(Peaks[Height]) > 8000, 1.8, 1.0)
RETURN
DIVIDE(Fatalities, TotalClimbers, 0) * 100 * AltitudeWeight

5. Key Findings

  • Age & Experience Bounds: Climbers above age 50 without prior high-altitude ascents represent a 3.5x hike in cardiac and exhaustion fatalities.
  • Summit Day Congestion: Bottlenecks above the Hillary Step (Mt. Everest) during high-pressure days increase severe frostbite occurrences and oxygen depletion by 40%.
  • Support Ratios: Standard expeditions with a support ratio of under 1:1 Sherpa-to-climber suffer double the casualty index of fully-guided configurations.

6. Recommendations & Impact

Actuary Decisions: Established dynamic underwriting premium bands linked to the HAFI score. We recommended introducing a mandatory "Previous Altitude Ascents" audit checkpoint before authorizing high-altitude risk coverage.

Business Impact: Reduced the underwriter claims outflow by 14% on extreme 8,000m expeditions while expanding policy sales volume on highly-regulated, safer peaks by 18%.

Broker Commission Audit & Distribution Intelligence

Domain: Sales Operations Tech Stack: SQL, Power BI, Excel Audit Project Scale: $12.4M Annual Premium

1. Business Challenge

A regional general insurance group operated complex multi-channel distribution agreements comprising agency networks, independent brokers, and direct channels. Inefficient commission accounting scripts paid out incentives based on forecasted premium invoices rather than paid-in cash flows. This resulted in significant commission overpayments on policies that were later canceled, lapsed, or modified.

2. Data Sources & Integration

Reconciled tables from the billing ledger with sales agency listings and the broker distribution catalog. Schema records analyzed:

Premium Ledger Policy ID, Premium Invoice, Paid Status, Payment Date
Broker Incentive Rates Broker ID, Channel Type, LOB rate, Incentive Schedule

3. Analytical Approach

I built a SQL reconciliation script that isolated historical discrepancies between invoice totals and cash settlements. This query identified broker accounts with excessive payouts on canceled and outstanding invoices.

SQL Code designed to calculate commission leakage by Broker Channel:

WITH PremiumRecon AS (
  SELECT p.BrokerID, p.PolicyID, p.PremiumInvoiced,
         p.PremiumPaid, b.CommissionRate,
         (p.PremiumInvoiced * b.CommissionRate) AS PaidCommission,
         (p.PremiumPaid * b.CommissionRate) AS EarnedCommission
  FROM PremiumLedger p
  JOIN BrokerIncentives b ON p.BrokerID = b.BrokerID
)
SELECT BrokerID, SUM(PremiumInvoiced) AS Invoiced,
       SUM(PremiumPaid) AS Paid,
       SUM(PaidCommission - EarnedCommission) AS CommissionLeakage
FROM PremiumRecon
GROUP BY BrokerID
HAVING SUM(PaidCommission - EarnedCommission) > 0;

5. Key Findings

  • Commission Leakage: Intermediary broker accounts generated 28% of lapsed policy cancellations but received full initial commission payments, resulting in $280k in direct annual leakage.

6. Recommendations & Impact

Policy Reform: Advised implementing "Commission clawback rules" inside active broker agreements, where commissions are settled strictly in installments matching premium cash inflows.

Business Impact: Recovered $115k in active commission discrepancies and audited a total of $280k in annual leakage savings.

Bancassurance Performance Funnel Optimization

Domain: Commercial Integration Tech Stack: PostgreSQL, Power BI, ETL Project Scale: 45,800 Active Policies

1. Business Challenge

Consolidating and auditing real-time performance indicators across 12 partner banks was highly complex. The bank networks transferred sales performance data via varying excel formats on bi-weekly lags. This latency meant sales directors had no visibility into branch conversion trends or partner productivity levels, and could not address drop-offs in the pipeline.

2. Data Sources

Built a secure staging schema mapping pipeline records from Standard, Apex, and Elite Bank networks:

Sales Pipelines ID, BankName, Branch, AgentID, LeadStage, DateCreated, Value
Outstanding Claims ID, PolicyID, BankSource, LossAmount, OutstandingReserves

3. Analytical Approach

We designed an automated ETL workflow using Python to ingest bank CSV files, parse schemas, and load them into a unified PostgreSQL staging schema. Power BI metrics were constructed using custom DAX variables to monitor sales funnels and partner agent performance scores.

Conversion_Rate =
VAR CreatedLeads = COUNTROWS(Pipelines)
VAR ConvertedLeads = CALCULATE(COUNTROWS(Pipelines), Pipelines[Stage] = "Converted")
RETURN
DIVIDE(ConvertedLeads, CreatedLeads, 0)

Spotify Listening Behavior Research

Domain: User Experience Tech Stack: Python, SQL, Power BI Project Scale: 840 Listening Hours

1. Business Challenge

Understanding acoustic user engagement profiles and identifying skip triggers in song playbacks. By conducting research over personal listening histories (tracks, skip records, play durations, devices), I aimed to establish data-driven rules for compiling high-retention acoustic playlists.

2. Data Sources

Extracted listening logs in JSON from Spotify account files, parsed metadata, and loaded records into a staging database:

# Parse Spotify listening histories in Python
import pandas as pd
df = pd.read_json("ListeningHistory.json")
df['PlayDuration_Min'] = df['msPlayed'] / 60000
df['IsSkipped'] = df.apply(lambda row: row['PlayDuration_Min'] < 0.5, axis=1)
df.to_csv("ParsedStreams.csv", index=False)
